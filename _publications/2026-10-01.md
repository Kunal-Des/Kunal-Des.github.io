WR 6 is the prototype for periodic variability due to large-scale wind structures in Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars, showing a consistent periodicity of 3.76 d in photometry, spectroscopy, and polarimetry. However, the origin of this variability is debated, attributed either to corotating interaction regions (CIRs) or a low-mass companion. We use time-resolved long-baseline interferometry to investigate the origin of periodic variability in WR 6. Inspired by recent interferometric observations that spatially resolved the stellar winds of WR stars, we aim to detect and characterize structures in the stellar winds of WR 6 and discriminate between the CIR and low-mass companion scenarios. Using the extended configuration of the Auxiliary Telescopes at VLTI, we obtained six GRAVITY epochs over six months to search for the presence and variability of spatially resolved wind-structures in WR 6. We also obtained complementary spectroscopic observations during one of the epochs to monitor contemporaneous spectral line variability. For all six epochs of GRAVITY observations: (i) WR 6 is consistent with a single star and has no spatially resolved companion detected above our sensitivity limit of $$M\gtrsim 6\,M_\odot$$; (ii) The He i line-emitting region is spatially resolved, while the He ii lines remain unresolved, consistent with atmosphere models for WR 6; (iii) Around 2.19 $$\mu$$m, one or more discrete line-emitting features offset from the center of the star are detected. The number, positions, and wavelengths of these features vary from epoch to epoch. Spectroscopic monitoring of WR 6 around the time of the fifth epoch reveals spectral line residuals that broadly correspond to the two features seen in the GRAVITY data of the fifth epoch. Finally, the first three epochs, which were observed on three consecutive nights, show significant variability in these features but cannot be directly linked across epochs. We detect multiple large-scale structures in the wind of WR 6 with VLTI/GRAVITY. We favor CIRs as their likely origin owing to temporal and spatial variability that cannot be reconciled with a low-mass companion. Building upon the first VLTI detection of CIRs, we encourage future campaigns with higher cadence to constrain the time-evolution and geometry of CIRs.
