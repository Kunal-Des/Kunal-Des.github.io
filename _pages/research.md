#Wolf-Rayet stars
======
Wolf-Rayet (WR) stars in the most general sense are stars with spectra dominated by emission lines of helium along with metals such as nitrogen, carbon and oxygen. In the context of massive stars, the so-called classical WRs are evolved, stripped cores of stars with initial masses $$M_{\rm ini}\gtrsim 20\,M_\odot$$. They are typically hydrogen-poor due to stripping either by their own stellar winds or a binary companion. They are also thought to be immediate progenitors of stellar-mass black holes.
We now know that essentially all massive stars are born in binary or multiple systems, based on several studies targeting massive stars in the Milky Way as well as the Magellanic Clouds. Furthermore, about 70% are likely to interact (e.g. tides, mass transfer, mergers) with their companions during their lifetime. As a result, binary evolution is expected to play an important role in the formation of WRs. Inversely, understanding binary properties of the WR population can provide key constraints on binary interactions. 
The rising population of merging stellar-mass black holes detected by LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA have prompted efforts to understand how they form, with massive binary evolution considered a leading formation channel.
